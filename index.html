Preface

Artificial intelligence is undergoing a profound paradigm shift. With the rise of large language models (LLMs), we are no longer merely building systems that can interpret text. Instead, we are beginning to build systems that can perceive, reason, plan, and act in complex environments. These systems are commonly referred to as agents.

Agents represent a new stage in the evolution of AI. Traditional machine learning models largely functioned as static predictors: they received an input and produced an output. Agents, in contrast, operate as dynamic decision systems. They can plan tasks, invoke tools, maintain memory, interact with external environments, and collaborate with other agents. Rather than producing a single answer, they participate in an ongoing loop of perception, reasoning, and action.

This shift is fundamentally changing how AI applications are designed. Instead of building isolated models, we are now designing systems composed of models, tools, memory, and interaction protocols. Understanding this transition is essential for anyone who wants to work with modern AI systems.

The Purpose of This Book

The goal of this book is to provide a systematic understanding of modern AI agents.

Many discussions of agents focus either on high-level concepts or on specific frameworks and code examples. This book takes a different approach. Its primary goal is to help readers understand how agent systems work, why they are designed the way they are, and how the different components fit together into a coherent architecture.

Code examples are included throughout the book, but they serve mainly as illustrations of key ideas, not as the central focus. The emphasis is on building a clear conceptual and architectural understanding that allows readers to reason about agent systems independently of any particular framework.

By the end of this book, readers should not only know how to build simple agents, but more importantly how to think about agent systems.

Structure of the Book

This book is organized into four parts, gradually moving from foundational concepts to system-level design.

Part I — Foundations and Background (Chapters 1–3)

Chapter 1 begins with a fundamental question: What is an agent?

It introduces the basic definition of agents, different types of agent systems, and the core perception–reasoning–action loop that underlies agent behavior. A minimal example is provided to build an intuitive understanding of how an agent operates.

Chapter 2 traces the historical evolution of agent ideas. From early symbolic AI and expert systems, through connectionist approaches and reinforcement learning, to modern LLM-driven agents, the chapter highlights the conceptual shifts that shaped today’s agent systems.

Chapter 3 provides the necessary background on large language models. It explains the evolution from n-gram models to Transformers and discusses key concepts such as decoder-only architectures, tokenization, prompt engineering, and scaling laws. These ideas form the technical foundation for understanding LLM-based agents.

Part II — Agent Paradigms and Frameworks (Chapters 4 and 6)

Chapter 4 explores several influential agent paradigms, including ReAct (Reason + Act) and Plan-and-Solve. These paradigms illustrate how reasoning and tool usage can be structured into coherent workflows.

Chapter 6 introduces several widely used agent frameworks, including AutoGen, AgentScope, CAMEL, and LangGraph. Rather than focusing on detailed tutorials, the chapter examines their design principles, architectural differences, and appropriate use cases.

Part III — Core Capabilities and System Architecture (Chapters 8–10)

Chapter 8 focuses on memory and retrieval systems, which provide the persistent knowledge needed for long-running agents. The chapter explains different types of memory, retrieval mechanisms, and the role of memory in agent reasoning.

Chapter 9 examines context engineering, a concept that has become central to modern agent design. The chapter reframes context not merely as input text, but as the operating environment of an agent—a first-class design element that must be constructed, maintained, and optimized.

Chapter 10 discusses agent communication protocols, including Model Context Protocol (MCP), Agent-to-Agent communication (A2A), and emerging approaches to agent network interoperability. These protocols are essential for building scalable multi-agent systems.

Part IV — Advanced Topics (Chapters 11–12)

Chapter 11 explores how agents can improve through interaction and feedback. It introduces the role of reinforcement learning and discusses the challenges involved in training agents in complex environments.

Chapter 12 addresses the problem of agent evaluation, which is significantly more complex than evaluating traditional models. The chapter analyzes what should be evaluated, common evaluation strategies, and the difficulties involved in assessing open-ended agent behavior.

What Makes This Book Different

This book emphasizes understanding over implementation.

Rather than presenting a collection of framework tutorials, it focuses on the underlying ideas that make agent systems possible. Readers will learn how to reason about agents at the levels of architecture, capability, and system design.

Code examples are used to clarify concepts and demonstrate practical implementations, but the primary objective is to develop a clear mental model of agent systems.

By understanding the principles behind agents, readers will be better prepared to adapt to new tools, frameworks, and research developments as the field continues to evolve.

Intended Audience

This book is intended for readers who want to understand the emerging field of AI agents, including:

  • Developers who want a systematic introduction to agent systems

  • Engineers working with large language models who want to move beyond simple prompting

  • Researchers exploring the architecture of modern AI systems

  • Anyone curious about how AI is evolving from prediction systems to decision-making systems

Basic programming experience (especially Python) will be helpful, but deep prior knowledge of machine learning is not required.

How to Use This Book

The book can be read sequentially from beginning to end, as each chapter builds on the concepts introduced earlier.

Readers who want a quick overview of agents may start with Chapters 1 and 4. Those interested in the technical foundations of language models may wish to read Chapter 3 in detail. Readers focused on system design will find Chapters 8–10 particularly relevant.

A Field in Motion

The field of AI agents is evolving rapidly. New frameworks, protocols, and architectures appear frequently. Rather than attempting to capture a fixed snapshot of the field, this book aims to provide conceptual tools for understanding its ongoing evolution.

Agents mark an important transition in artificial intelligence: from systems that merely interpret information to systems that can act within the world.

Understanding this transition is the first step toward building the intelligent systems of the future.
